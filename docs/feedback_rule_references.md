# Feedback Rule References

This table explains the educational concepts that inform EssayScaff's student-facing feedback. **None of the cited work validates EssayScaff's particular spaCy heuristics, thresholds, messages, or student outcomes.** Published and university writing guidance informs the criteria students are invited to review; the implementation rules remain local heuristics that report observable signals.

## Rule-to-reference mapping

| Writing Criterion | Existing Rule ID | What It Detects | Academic Reference | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Main-point / thesis review in an Introduction | `INTRO.CLAUSE.SUBJECT_PREDICATE_CANDIDATE`; `INTRO.CLAUSE.NO_SUBJECT_PREDICATE_CANDIDATE` | spaCy dependency/POS conditions for a finite root clause with a subject, or no such candidate | The Writing Center, University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill (n.d.), [Thesis Statements](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/thesis-statements/) — institutional writing guidance on a thesis as a claim/argument | The rule detects sentence structure, not a thesis, claim, position, or the meaning of a proposition. A grammatical nonsense sentence may qualify; valid stylistic openings may not. This rule is an implementation heuristic. |
| Assignment-topic overlap in the Introduction | `INTRO.TOPIC.FOCUS_TERMS_OBSERVED`; `INTRO.TOPIC.NO_FOCUS_MATCH_OBSERVED` | Exact or lemma-normalized spans for configured focus terms, or no configured focus span | UNC Writing Center (n.d.), [Thesis Statements](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/thesis-statements/) explains that an introduction's thesis/claim should respond to the assignment focus | Matching words do not prove relevance. A missing match does not prove the text is off-topic because paraphrases can be missed. This is an implementation heuristic. |
| Broad focus-term qualifier | `INTRO.TOPIC.FOCUS_TERMS_OBSERVED` | For student-facing phrasing only, distinguishes matches limited to `student(s)` or `use` forms from other configured terms. Underlying TopicService matches are unchanged. | No academic source validates this small lexical breadth list; it is a conservative feedback wording heuristic prompted by the test case | This list does not decide semantic relevance. It only prevents broad-word matches from being described as sufficient topic alignment. |
| Possible main-point / thesis review | `INTRO.CLAUSE.SUBJECT_PREDICATE_CANDIDATE`; `INTRO.CLAUSE.NO_SUBJECT_PREDICATE_CANDIDATE` | Existing spaCy dependency/POS conditions for a finite root clause and subject, or no such observed structure | UNC Writing Center (n.d.), [Thesis Statements](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/thesis-statements/) describes a thesis as a claim/argument that responds to an assignment | This rule detects sentence structure, not a thesis, claim, position, or meaning. A grammatical nonsense sentence may qualify; valid stylistic openings may not. This is an implementation heuristic. |
| Additional opening development / reason review | `INTRO.DEVELOPMENT.MULTIPLE_CLAUSE_PREDICATES`; `INTRO.DEVELOPMENT.NO_ADDITIONAL_CLAUSE_CANDIDATE` | Counts existing finite clause-predicate candidates and provides predicate spans; used for an optional question about whether a reason is explained | UNC Writing Center (n.d.), [Paragraphs](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/paragraphs/) describes elaboration, examples, and explanation as ways to develop ideas | Predicate counts do not identify persuasive reasons or assess meaning. A concise opening can be sufficient. This is an implementation heuristic, not a validated development measure. |
| Evidence-linked formative feedback | `INTRO.GOAL.OBSERVABLE_INTRODUCTION` plus the existing rule IDs associated with each feedback field in `Feedback Provenance` | Links feedback fields to existing triggered events and actual evidence | Hattie, J., & Timperley, H. (2007), “The Power of Feedback,” *Review of Educational Research, 77*(1), 81–112. [https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430298487](https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430298487); Knight, S., Shibani, A., Abel, S., Gibson, A. A. P., & Ryan, P. (2020), “AcaWriter: A Learning Analytics Tool for Formative Feedback on Academic Writing,” *Journal of Writing Research, 12*(1), 141–186. [https://doi.org/10.17239/jowr-2020.12.01.06](https://doi.org/10.17239/jowr-2020.12.01.06) | These works inform formative feedback design but do not validate EssayScaff's implementation. No AcaWriter code or rules are copied or adapted. |
| Body paragraph boundaries | `BODY.P{n}.PARAGRAPH.SEGMENTED` | Splits text on blank lines and returns trimmed original-text spans | No academic source validates the blank-line segmentation policy; it is an input-format convention | A detected text block is not proof of rhetorical paragraph quality. |
| Body topic-sentence candidates | `BODY.P{n}.TOPIC_SENTENCE.CANDIDATE`; `BODY.P{n}.TOPIC_SENTENCE.NO_CANDIDATE` | Requires a parser clause candidate and either a focus-term match in that sentence or a concept recurring across sentences | The Writing Center, UNC Chapel Hill (n.d.), [Paragraphs](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/paragraphs/) describes a controlling idea often expressed by a topic sentence | The implementation does not establish the sentence's rhetorical role. UNC guidance does not validate these lexical/POS conditions. |
| Body supporting-detail candidates | `BODY.P{n}.SUPPORTING_DETAIL.CANDIDATE`; `BODY.P{n}.SUPPORTING_DETAIL.NOT_OBSERVED` | Uses a threshold of two parser-labeled clause candidates in one paragraph | UNC Writing Center (n.d.), [Paragraphs](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/paragraphs/) discusses explanation, evidence, and detail as paragraph development | The parser candidate count is a local structural heuristic, not evidence that a claim is supported. |
| Body example/explanation markers | `BODY.P{n}.EXAMPLE_OR_EXPLANATION_MARKER` | Searches for `for example`, `for instance`, `such as`, or `including` | UNC Writing Center (n.d.), [Paragraphs](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/paragraphs/) includes an illustration/development example | A marker does not establish that a relevant example or explanation follows. |
| Adjacent-sentence lexical observations | `BODY.P{n}.UNITY.SENTENCE_PAIR_{i}_{j}` | Records shared case-folded content words and shared spaCy lemmas for adjacent sentence pairs | Witte, S. P., & Faigley, L. (1981), “Coherence, Cohesion, and Writing Quality,” *College Composition and Communication, 32*(2), 189–204. [https://doi.org/10.58680/ccc198115912](https://doi.org/10.58680/ccc198115912); UNC Writing Center (n.d.), [Paragraphs](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/paragraphs/) | These sources discuss writing constructs; they do not validate surface/lemma overlap as a unity classifier. |
| Repetition and sentence-opening observations | `BODY.P{n}.REPETITION.*`; `CONCLUSION.REPETITION.*` | Counts repeated content surfaces, normalized lemmas, and two-word sentence openings | Witte & Faigley (1981) is relevant research context for cohesion and writing quality | Repetition may support continuity; these counts do not establish a defect or quality score. |
| Adjacent-paragraph lexical/transition comparison | `BODY.CONTINUITY.ADJACENT_PARAGRAPH_COMPARISON` | Compares neighboring substantive paragraph concepts and scans the next paragraph for a fixed transition-expression list | Witte & Faigley (1981); UNC Writing Center (n.d.), [Paragraphs](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/paragraphs/) discusses transitions and logical progression | This fixed lexical list is an EssayScaff heuristic. A transition word does not prove coherence; paraphrases can be missed. |
| Introduction–Body and prior-section lexical comparisons | `BODY.INTRO_CONTINUITY.LEXICAL_COMPARISON`; `CONCLUSION.CONTINUITY.*` | Compares extracted normalized concepts and, in separate optional calls, WordNet candidate relationships | Witte & Faigley (1981) provides broad coherence/cohesion research context; Princeton WordNet and NLTK documentation describe the lexical resource and API | No cited source validates this implementation as thesis support, contradiction, synthesis, or contextual semantic relatedness. |
| Conclusion synthesis structure | `CONCLUSION.SYNTHESIS.STRUCTURE_CANDIDATE`; `CONCLUSION.SYNTHESIS.NO_CANDIDATE` | Requires a parser-labeled clause containing at least two normalized concepts observed in supplied prior sections | No source validates this exact two-concept threshold as a synthesis detector | This is only a cross-stage lexical-plus-clause candidate; it does not assess how ideas are connected. |
| Conclusion closure markers | `CONCLUSION.CLOSURE.STRUCTURE_CANDIDATE`; `CONCLUSION.CLOSURE.NO_CANDIDATE`; `CONCLUSION.CLOSURE.MODAL_OR_RECOMMENDATION_CUE` | Uses final-root-clause position, a fixed closing-expression list, and final-sentence cue words | No cited source validates these phrases or positions as effective closure | Genres differ; a cue is not proof of rhetorical effectiveness. |
| Conclusion unobserved concepts | `CONCLUSION.NEW_MATERIAL.UNOBSERVED_CONCEPTS`; `CONCLUSION.NEW_MATERIAL.KNOWN_CONCEPT_COMBINATION` | Compares normalized extracted concepts and content lemmas across supplied sections | Witte & Faigley (1981) is broad context for relations across text; no direct source validates this method | A phrase absent earlier is not necessarily a new argument; paraphrase and concept extraction limits apply. |

## References and provenance notes

- Hattie and Timperley (2007) is used as a broad source on feedback for learning, not as evidence that EssayScaff feedback is effective.
- Knight et al. (2020) documents AcaWriter as a learning analytics tool for formative feedback on academic writing. It is cited as a related design approach only; EssayScaff does not install AcaWriter or reuse its code.
- UNC Writing Center handouts are university writing guidance, not peer-reviewed empirical validation. They are cited for thesis and paragraph-development concepts, not for EssayScaff's computational detection.
- The broad-focus qualifier changes only student-facing feedback wording; the TopicService matching results and Rule Bases are unchanged.
- Existing code identifiers and evidence remain the source of each detection warning. The Research Inspector adds tracing and explanations; it does not introduce or change Rule Base conditions or NLP algorithms.
- The Body and Conclusion mappings above identify writing concepts that motivate review prompts; none validates the exact EssayScaff parser conditions, lexical lists, thresholds, or student outcomes.
- Technical implementation references used by the Research Inspector include the [spaCy processing-pipeline guide](https://spacy.io/usage/processing-pipelines), the [spaCy Token API](https://spacy.io/api/token), [NLTK WordNet usage](https://www.nltk.org/howto/wordnet.html), and [Princeton WordNet](https://wordnet.princeton.edu/). These document APIs/data concepts, not the accuracy of EssayScaff's custom heuristics.
